#+title: ADR-0018: Bounded v5 Normative Authority Reset
#+date: 2026-07-28
#+status: Accepted

* Context

ADR-0014 and the D3 completion plan preserve continuous normative ownership
for all 121 v4.9 requirement identities while =docs/design.md= is decomposed
one domain at a time.  That model prevents duplicate or missing owners, but it
also assumes that every residual v4.9 clause remains a justified v1 obligation
until migrated.

Repository evidence no longer supports that assumption.  Only 34 requirements
are already at their final owners, three are owned by registered amendments,
and 84 remain in the 4,105-line monolith.  The first ten-requirement Foundation
candidate alone changes 21 paths, adds roughly 1,186 lines, and deletes or
rewrites roughly 453 lines without changing implementation.  Repeating that
protocol across the remaining target domains would consume review capacity
while the first production-boundary Property Read composition is still blocked
at the host-erased/static compiler-artifact boundary.

The opposite extreme is also unsafe.  Treating all residual text as irrelevant
would silently discard lifecycle, cleanup, generation, resource, portability,
security, and protocol-neutrality constraints on which completed evidence and
the Property Read gate depend.

=workspace/0015-normative-authority-reset.md= therefore classifies every v4.9
requirement using repository architecture, accepted ADRs, completed evidence,
the Property Read gate, work-package entry conditions, machine-readable
contracts, and current implementation.  The classification is exact and
expands to all 121 identities once:

- 41 indispensable current architecture or safety invariants;
- 21 requirements directly required by the Property Read composition;
- 34 declared-v1 requirements that are not needed by the current vertical
  slice;
- 15 useful deferred design inputs;
- four premature or superseded freezes; and
- six requirements made redundant by stronger governance or executable
  owners.

The exact membership, counts, inactive dispositions, reset basis, and withheld
activation state are registered in =docs/spec/v5-authority-reset.toml=. That
manifest, not the summary counts alone, is the transition projection consumed
by the candidate review.

* Decision

The project will supersede the v4.9 residual-decomposition model with a v5.0
bounded-core authority reset.

The v5.0 active requirement set contains exactly the 62 requirements in the
first two classes above.  A requirement is active only when it satisfies at
least one of these objective rules:

1. removing it would reopen a current ownership, lifecycle, cleanup,
   generation, resource, security, portability, protocol-neutrality, or
   external-standard invariant; or
2. the registered Property Read integration gate or completed implementation
   evidence directly depends on it.

The 34 declared-v1 requirements remain release obligations but are inactive
until their owning domain approaches implementation.  Each affected
work-package entry must re-evaluate and either re-adopt, replace, or explicitly
retire the relevant requirements before implementation admission.  They do not
silently regain authority because a historical clause or work-package list
mentions them.

The 15 useful deferred inputs remain searchable historical design material.
The four premature or superseded freezes are not v1 commitments.  The six
redundant requirements are discharged by their stronger governance,
architecture, manifest, checker, or machine-readable owners rather than copied
into another prose specification.

The authority reset is a new design revision.  It MUST NOT masquerade as an
ordinary v4.9 D3 domain migration.  Until one exact v5.0 activation candidate
passes independent review and is integrated atomically, v4.9 remains the
active authority and no partial v5.0 file grants implementation admission.

The activated v5.0 hierarchy is:

1. =docs/design.md= selects v5.0 and lists the complete active normative set;
2. the architecture backbone owns cross-domain flows, boundaries, and
   invariants;
3. accepted ADRs own durable decisions and rationale;
4. registered domain specifications and still-needed narrow amendments own
   exact behavior and public/internal contracts;
5. API ownership, state, resource, requirement, performance, and work-package
   registries own their machine-readable projections; and
6. source and tests remain implementation truth and executable conformance.

The activated requirement registry MUST distinguish active requirements from
declared-v1 deferred, design-input, retired, and redundant identities.  The
v4.9 source location and reset-base Git reference remain recorded for every
inactive identity.  The D3 final-target DAG and its migration-only activation
checks are retired; they are not replaced by another lossless-decomposition
DAG.

Completed WP-000 and WP-100 evidence is neither rewritten nor silently carried
forward.  The v5.0 activation review must check each completed evidence record
against the 62-requirement set and record one of =reaffirmed=, =superseded=, or
=historical=.  Existing implementation remains current only where the review
finds its exact public and semantic contract unchanged.

The Foundation authority candidate
=2494f33fdfe49ec3c7ae850d20990e446e628865= is abandoned as an activation
candidate.  Its Foundation specification and reasoning may be reused as v5.0
migration input, but its 21-path D3 boundary, 44/76/1 count transition, and
independent-D3 integration protocol MUST NOT be activated.  The candidate and
base hashes remain historical rollback and audit references.

WP-200 design work may resume only after both of these conditions hold:

1. the exact v5.0 authority-reset candidate has passed independent review and
   activated atomically; and
2. =workspace/0014-property-read-plan-artifact-boundary.md= has frozen one
   constructible host-erased/static compiler and artifact representation,
   single implementation ownership, and paired third-party authoring fixtures.

No planning, Core, TD, binding, Servient, or Property Read architecture-fixture
implementation is admitted by this ADR.

* Activation and rollback

One immutable activation candidate must contain the complete revision switch:

- concise v5.0 design and specification indexes;
- the classified requirement registry and exact 62 active definitions;
- removal of residual authority from =docs/design.md=;
- retirement of the D3 decomposition index and migration-only checks;
- v5.0 projections in architecture, ADR, artifact, governance, work-package,
  gate, plan, and continuation records;
- explicit completed-evidence dispositions;
- a checker proving the 121-way classification, 62-way active ownership, no
  duplicate active definitions, no inactive implementation admission, and the
  exact candidate boundary; and
- passing aggregate design, work-package, completed-tranche, workspace, and
  feature checks required by the candidate review.

The candidate changes no runtime or public API.  Independent review compares
the candidate to its frozen mainline parent.  Activation is a separate
integration checkpoint and may not add or alter candidate paths.

Rollback before activation is simply refusal to integrate the candidate;
v4.9 remains active.  After activation, the exact pre-activation mainline
commit is the rollback point.  Rollback is an explicit revision change and
must also restore the v4.9 registry/checker set atomically; individual v5.0
files may not be reverted piecemeal.

* Consequences

- Current active requirement count falls from 121 to 62 without losing the
  exact history or the v1-deferred queue.
- Review effort is concentrated on contracts that protect current code and the
  first executable cross-package composition.
- Later domains cannot inherit speculative v4.9 detail accidentally; their
  entry review must justify the contract that becomes active.
- The Property Read path remains protected by lifecycle, cleanup, resource,
  generation, no-runtime-TD-read, host/static, and protocol-neutral
  requirements.
- Existing machine-readable resource and performance artifacts may remain
  stronger owners without duplicating their policy as requirement prose.
- v5.0 activation itself remains a Category C architecture change requiring an
  exact candidate, independent review, and recoverable checkpoint.

* Rejected alternatives

- Continuing D3 unchanged was rejected because its repeated authority-only
  migration and review cost is not bounded by implementation risk and does not
  shorten the Property Read critical path.
- A full reset with no selective carry-forward was rejected because it would
  silently invalidate completed evidence and remove indispensable safety and
  ownership constraints.
- Calling the reset another v4.9 migration was rejected because it changes the
  meaning of active requirement identity, supersedes ADR-0014's transition
  model, and invalidates the D3 completion DAG.
- Retaining a requirement merely because a checker, work package, or old
  identifier mentions it was rejected; active authority requires one of the
  two objective carry-forward rules above.
