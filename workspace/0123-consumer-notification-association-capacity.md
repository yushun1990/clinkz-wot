# Continuing Consumer notification association and capacity

Status: MIGRATED

Review 10's notification root combined three unrelated lifetimes: qualified
preparation, one Consumer's policy/authority association, and vacant conversation
capacity. Its preparation also recharged aliased service backing per definition
(F2); runtime construction adopted a newly read data policy (F3). Existing MQTT
interests already separate logical participation from native topics/connections.
Those native leases are useful evidence, not a reason to preserve the Engine root.

The selected increment retains a qualified endpoint-specialized notification
mapping once, binds independent Consumer references/access owners/instance banks,
and supplies family-specific work capacity only at subscribe. The raw lifecycle
slab remains the work owner; each admitted entry receives its own source-free
context. A passive association may outlive capacity or be used in several capacity
domains, subject to its actual registration, currentness and account limits.
Original work retains the association after application view loss. This mirrors
the finite separation without making a continuing conversation a finite call.

Compared alternatives:

- Rebuilding a complete old Runtime on each use avoids some idle storage but
  repeats its binding/owner setup and couples its disposal to accepted work.
- Wrapping one old root per persistent association preserves prepaid idle slots.
- One raw root per admitted conversation avoids that coupling but repeats slab,
  issuer and root bookkeeping. Supplying the original context to each entry is
  the smaller underlying correction and keeps existing cleanup mechanisms.
- A universal Runtime, Engine subscription pool, scheduler or shared session type
  adds no capability needed by this counterexample. MQTT keeps its native owners.
- Recompiling/interning definitions after each Consumer repeats the work to reuse.
  Binding the same actual immutable mapping needs dependency checks, policy and
  record funding, without TD admission or lowering. Changing its endpoint needs
  separately qualified preparation; HTTP's dependency extractor is not a universal
  MQTT or cross-family compiler representation.

Data/access backing imports the existing physical service owners. Preparation
captures its data/media/native support declarations; explicit association binding
qualifies its access owner and captures its work/revision. Capacity construction
reads none of these as a new baseline. Raw startup adoption recognizes aliases
within a registry; separate registries must propagate the same retained owners.

Policy receives the original conversation and optional occurrence coordinate.
This corrects the inability to distinguish named collection Events at acceptance,
delayed delivery and response. A coordinate is not authenticated source evidence.
A verifier-backed source claim, its trust/dependency revision, bounded owned
context and forged/absent/ambiguous/stale-proof tests remain a separate capability.
NoSec MQTT cannot supply that proof, even if a topic or payload labels a meter.

Contracts are migrated to the existing Planning, Core, Binding, Foundation and
Runtime Safety owners, their API/state projections and work packages. Evidence
is in the notification composition record. Action F2/F3, broader checked lowering,
managed all-family registration, automatic progress and constrained notification
execution remain outside this increment. No milestone or gate closes.

## Subscription authority and individual occurrence refusal

The first policy expression conflated the continuing grant with item permission:
Deliver was called without an item before native polling, and any refusal fenced
the whole conversation. A policy requiring an actual Event could not reach its
allowed input; a denied queued Event prevented later allowed Events on the same
subscription. Real MQTT counterexamples falsify that contract.

The selected correction adds a subscription Maintain check and the explicit
Allow/RejectOccurrence disposition for actual Deliver/Respond decisions. Policy
errors still fence authority; there is no inference from generic security/error
codes. Item refusal uses the existing staged/queue owner and one irreversible
flag, then the same bounded reply/payload retirement mechanics as close. The
participation and continuing identity survive successful item retirement. Required
replies receive no fabricated response; native retirement failures remain fatal.

Treating every error as an item refusal would hide dependency/authority loss.
Reusing Establish as the continuing phase obscures the independent lifetime.
Per-Event subscriptions or rewriting MQTT interests would change collection and
native ownership. A generic filtering pipeline, new queue or pool adds no needed
capability. The original F2/F3 preparation/owner/funding boundaries are retained.
Exact semantics are migrated to Core/Runtime Safety; the same evidence record
links native, queued-policy-change and response-owner counterexamples. No topic
or NoSec payload becomes authenticated source evidence.
